# Books to read — motivation restart

Read in this order. Do not jump ahead. One at a time.

---

## 1. The War of Art — Steven Pressfield (2002)
- **Read now.** Short, brutal, no theory.
- Names your exact state as "Resistance" — the thing that stops you sitting down.
- 10 pages is enough to want to open the tracker again.
- Status: [ ] not started

## 2. 168 Hours — Laura Vanderkam (2010, paperback 2011)
- The only one actually about **time as a budget**, not productivity tricks.
- Makes you log one honest week and see where it went.
- You already have the tool for this — log the week in Tenfold while you read it.
- No revised edition exists. Data is 2010-era, the method is not.
- Status: [ ] not started

## 3. Atomic Habits — James Clear (2018)
- Read **alongside** #2, not after.
- Only two chapters matter right now: the 2-minute rule, and never-miss-twice.
- Skip the rest until the streak is back.
- Status: [ ] not started

## 4. Tranquility by Tuesday — Laura Vanderkam (2022)
- Same author as #2, newer, and rules instead of philosophy.
- 9 rules, each tested on ~150 real people before she published them.
- Examples: plan next week on Friday, three times a week counts as a habit, give yourself a bedtime.
- Closest thing here to something you can put straight into the tracker.
- Status: [ ] not started

## 5. Deep Work — Cal Newport (2016)
- Read this **after** motivation is back, when you want output not ignition.
- The rule: schedule the block, not the intention. Same idea as nuggets.
- Status: [ ] not started

## 6. Four Thousand Weeks — Oliver Burkeman (2021)
- **Only if** the first five make you feel worse.
- Opposite medicine — removes the guilt of being behind.
- Skip it if the guilt is not the problem.
- Status: [ ] not started

---

## Bench — not now

### Off the Clock — Laura Vanderkam (2018)
- About *feeling* less busy, not doing more. Time diaries from ~900 people.
- Read only if the problem turns out to be feeling rushed, not feeling unmotivated.
- Not your case right now.

---

## Rules for this list
- One book open at a time (exception: #2 and #3 together).
- 10 pages a day beats 100 pages a weekend.
- If a book is not working after 30 pages, drop it and move to the next one.
- Tick the status line when you finish. Do not add new books until #1–#5 are ticked.
