# Part 1 — The 9 rules from Tranquility by Tuesday

Laura Vanderkam, 2022. Each rule was tested on ~150 real people before she published it.

1. **Give yourself a bedtime** — same time every night unless there is a real reason not to.
2. **Plan on Fridays** — think through next week before you are inside it. Three lines: career, relationship, self.
3. **Move by 3 p.m.** — 10 minutes of physical activity in the first half of the day.
4. **Three times a week is a habit** — you do not need daily. Three times counts and it survives a bad week.
5. **Create a back-up slot** — for anything that matters, pick a second time in advance for when the first gets killed.
6. **One big adventure, one little adventure** — per week. Big is 3-4 hours, little is 1 hour.
7. **Take one night for you** — one weeknight that is yours, planned, not leftover.
8. **Batch the little things** — push small admin into one block so it does not spread across the day.
9. **Effortful before effortless** — reading and making come before scrolling, not after.

**The essence in one line:** your priorities do not need more hours, they need a **fixed slot and a back-up slot**.

# Part 2 — The rules built for your day

## The diagnosis
- You do not have a time problem. You have **five unowned hours** — 9:30 to 6:30 at home with only 3-4 hours of real work in it.
- Nothing is scheduled into that gap, so boredom fills it, and boredom picks the phone.
- Every broken habit — Sundarkand, temple, study — is downstream of that gap. Fix the gap, most of them come back on their own.

---

## Rule A — Two pillars, everything else floats
- Pick exactly **two** times in the day that never move: **9:00-9:35am Sundarkand** and **7:00-8:30pm temple**.
- Alarm at **8:55**, start by 8:57. Your call is at 9:35 — zero buffer if you start late, so the alarm is not optional.
- Two is the limit. A day with ten fixed things breaks by 11am, then you abandon all ten.
- Everything else floats around the pillars — study, Om, balayam, kids.
- If both pillars happened, the day counted. Even if nothing else did.
- Temple is **strict, no backup**. A miss is a miss — it does not carry to the next day, and it does not write off the week.

## Rule B — Check on the clock, not on the itch
*(rewritten for IT DevOps — you cannot avoid pings)*

- Being reachable is not being busy. DevOps makes you **available** for 9 hours, it does not make you **occupied** for 9 hours.
- The 30-minute check is not what breaks focus. What breaks it is the **20 minutes of drift after the check**. The check costs 3 minutes. The drift costs the afternoon.

1. **Checks happen at :00 and :30. Never in between.** Fixed clock times mean you are never deciding whether to check.
2. **A check is 3 minutes, with a timer.** Scan, triage, note it, close it. Start the timer *before* you look.
3. **Work in 25-minute chunks, not 2-hour blocks.** A 2-hour block cannot survive your job, so it fails, and then you stop trying.
4. **One 60-minute silent window a day.** Lowest-traffic hour. Notifications off. Tell the team: "heads-down 10-11, ping my phone if P1."
5. **P1 is the only thing allowed to break the rule.** Define it in writing, in advance. Most "urgent" pings are not urgent, they are just visible.
6. **Clock off at 6:30 even if you stay reachable.** Without a clock-off, the 7pm temple has nothing to start from. That is why it went.

## Rule C — The bell is the end, not a suggestion
- Your timer problem is not the timer. It is that **extending has no cost**, so it is always the easier choice.
- When the timer rings, you stop **mid-sentence**. Not at a good point. Mid-sentence.
- If the task is not done, log what you did and leave it. It goes to the next slot.
- Extending once teaches you the timer is fake, and then you stop trusting the whole system. That is what already happened.

## Rule D — Decide what boredom leads to, before you are bored
- Boredom is not the problem. Boredom is the **trigger**, and right now the only thing wired to it is the phone.
- Write five things, each under 20 minutes, that you do instead: Om 6 min, balayam 6 min, walk, one page of study, call someone.
- Keep the list **on paper**, near where you sit. Not in the phone. The phone is what you are escaping.
- The rule: **bored → look at the list, not the phone.** You are not fighting the urge, you are giving it somewhere else to go.

## Rule E — The 6-minute things never get their own slot
- Om 6 min and balayam 6 min will never happen as standalone appointments. Too small to remember, too small to feel important.
- **Attach them to something that already happens.** Om right after Sundarkand ends. Balayam while the kettle boils, or on the walk.
- Attached to an anchor = done daily without thinking. On a list = done never.

## Rule F — Put the kids in the schedule so they stop being the interruption
- They interrupt because they have no time of their own with you. Unscheduled attention gets taken at random.
- Give them a **named slot**, same time daily, 30-45 min. Teaching goes there.
- Then "not now, we have our time at X" is true, so it works.
- Tell them the slot exists. A child who knows their time is coming interrupts far less.

## Rule G — The app gets a box or it takes the day
- This is the single biggest leak, and it is the thing that broke a temple habit you held for years.
- **One named window, one day a week, with an end time.** Never in the hollow middle (9:30-6:30) — that is when you are bored, alone and unobserved, and that is when it eats whole afternoons.
- **Delete it from the phone between windows.** Reinstalling takes 2 minutes — that friction converts an impulse into a decision.
- **Never before 7pm.** Temple first. If the temple went, the day is already lost and the rest follows.
- Honest flag: if hours disappear even when you did not decide to open it, that is a compulsion loop, not a scheduling problem, and rules alone will not hold it. That is not a character thing — it is common and it responds well to talking to someone. Worth doing if two weeks of the box does not hold.

## Rule H — Every pillar gets a back-up slot
- Sundarkand: primary 9:00-9:35am, backup **inside the 7:00-8:30pm temple window** (same evening, no separate slot).
- Temple: primary 7:00-8:30pm, **no backup — strict by your choice.** One miss stays one miss, it does not chain.
- Decide the backup **now**, not on the day you miss. On the day you miss it you will not choose one, you will write the day off.
- One miss is normal. **Two in a row is what ends a habit.** The backup exists to stop the second one.

## Rule I — Three times a week is a habit, for everything that is not a pillar
- Study, extra goals, anything else: **3x a week**, not daily.
- Daily targets on five different things is why nothing sticks — one bad day breaks all five, so you drop all five.
- Two pillars daily. Everything else three times a week. That is a week you can actually hit.

# Part 3 — More on rules 6 and 7

## One big adventure, one little adventure

Why it works:
- Your brain stores **novelty**, not hours. A week of identical days compresses into one memory — that is why the last two months vanished.
- Two new things a week stretches the week back out.

The test: **could you describe it to someone a month later.** If no, it was not an adventure.

Does not count: the place you always go, shopping, errands, anything done while on the phone.

Little (1 hour): a street you never walked · somewhere new to eat, alone, phone away · a bookshop or library you never enter · sunrise or sunset with a view · a different route home with one stop · cook something new · one hour of a hobby you dropped.

Big (3-4 hours): a town 1-2 hours away · a long walk with a destination · a museum or market or old site in your own city you never visited · a one-off class · visit someone you have not seen in a year.

Make it happen:
- Keep a running **adventure list on paper**. Add to it whenever something comes to mind. Never plan from a blank page — that is why it fails.
- Little adventure is fixed to **Friday 17:00-18:00** (kids slot skipped that day, told in advance). Big adventure is fixed to the **weekend** (Saturday or Sunday, 3-4 hours) — day/time picked fresh each Thursday from the list.
- Booking or paying in advance makes it real. Free ones need a person attached.

## Take one night for you

Why it works:
- The problem is not that you have no free time. Your free time is **leftover** time — whatever is left at 10pm when you are already finished.
- Leftover time always becomes scrolling, because you have nothing left to decide with.
- A planned night is the same hours with the decision already made. That is the whole difference.

The rules of it:
- **One weeknight**, not a weekend. Fixed to **Tuesday, 21:00-22:00**, backup **Thursday, same time**.
- **Chosen before the week starts**, not discovered on the day.
- **Decide what you are doing on it** in advance. "Free night" fails. "Tuesday, reading, 8-10pm" works.
- **Tell whoever needs to know** so it does not get taken.

Put on it: reading, a course, the side project, training, music, writing. Or decided nothing — a bath, a film you chose, sitting outside.

Do not put on it: catching up on work, chores, admin. That is Rule 8.

Fails because:
- You picked the night that "looks free". Pick a normally-busy night and defend it — the free-looking night was always going to get taken.
- You did not decide what to do, so you scrolled. Decide on Thursday.
- One cancellation killed it. Give the night a **back-up night** too.

How 6 and 7 fit: kept **separate** here — little adventure (Friday) and your night (Tuesday) are two different slots, not merged. Thursday planning produces three things — big adventure, little adventure, your night. That is the week made memorable. Everything else is the other rules protecting those slots.

# Part 5 — The 4-week plan

Do not start nine rules at once. That is the same all-or-nothing that broke everything before.

## Week 1 — Only the two pillars
- Sundarkand 9:00-9:35 (alarm 8:55, backup inside temple window). Temple 7:00-8:30pm (strict, no backup). Nothing else changes.
- Success = both pillars hit 5 of 7 days. Not 7 of 7.
- Do NOT add study, do NOT fix the timer, do NOT touch the app rule yet.

## Week 2 — Add the clock-off and the checks
- Checks at :00 and :30 only, 3 minutes each, timer on.
- Clock off at 6:30 every day.
- Keep the pillars running. If a pillar slipped in week 1, repeat week 1 instead of moving on.
- Success = clock-off held 4 of 5 working days.

## Week 3 — Add the app box and the boredom list
- The app gets one named window, one day, with an end time. Never before 7pm. Deleted between windows.
- Boredom list written on paper and stuck near your desk.
- This is the hardest week. Expect to fail once — the point is not failing twice.
- Success = temple not missed because of the app, even once.

## Week 4 — Add the week-shaping rules
- Thursday planning, 16:35-17:00 (25 min): three lines (career/relationship/self) + pick big & little adventure from the list.
- Little adventure — Friday 17:00-18:00, kids slot skipped that day (tell them in advance).
- Big adventure — weekend (Sat or Sun), 3-4h, day/time picked fresh each Thursday.
- You-night — Tuesday 21:00-22:00, backup Thursday same time.
- Kids slot — daily 17:00-17:45 (except Friday).
- Success = the week has three things in it you could describe a month later.

## After week 4
- Add study and extra goals at **3x a week**, not daily.
- Silent window, admin batch, effortful-before-effortless go in only once the four weeks hold.
- If a week collapses, drop back one week. Never restart from zero.
